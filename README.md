### Reflection

**Situation:** Our ActiveEdge group needed a clear agreement on how we would work together before we started building the brand and website.

**Task:** As the team's Designer, I was responsible for the design, formatting and visual presentation of the charter so it was easy to read and use.

**Action:** The charter sets out the team mission and vision, the members with their roles (Group Leader, Researcher, Writer/Editor and Designer), and a communication plan: WhatsApp for day-to-day updates, Google Meet for meetings, Google Drive for shared documents, a weekly Monday check-in, and agreed response times. **Result:** The team had one reference point for roles, communication and goals, and the SMART goals table made progress visible, with most milestones marked as done or submitted. I learned how a charter reduces confusion in a group and how good formatting helps a document actually get used.

### Reflection

**Situation:** After the lecturer's feedback, we needed to turn what the project required into small pieces of work the team could finish and check off.

**Task:** I had to understand how to write user stories from the end-user's perspective and break each one into tasks.

**Action:** We wrote stories such as designing a professional logo that represents the ActiveEdge brand, sourcing high-quality product images, and expanding website sections based on feedback. On Trello, each card carries a checklist of tasks, so progress shows as a count such as 6/7 or 7/7.

**Result:** Splitting stories into tasks made the work manageable and made it clear who was doing what. I learned to describe features from the user's side instead of only listing technical jobs.

The product backlog is the prioritised list of everything the product needs. On our Trello board the **Product Backlog** column held 63 cards, labelled by sprint.

### Reflection

**Situation:** The project had a lot of work, from market research to branding and the website, and we needed to decide what came first.

**Task:** Our task was to collect all required work in one place and order it by priority.

**Action:** We put every item on the Trello board as a card with a sprint tag, for example the Sprint 4 cards to choose the brand colour palette and typography and to create a size guide, and the Sprint 3 card to design the apparel range. In the logbook taskboard the backlog items are grouped by sprint, such as Final Feedback &amp; Foundation and Visual Assets.

**Result:** The backlog gave the team a clear order of work and let us move cards forward as sprints started. I learned that a backlog is a living list that changes as feedback comes in.

The sprint backlog is the work picked from the product backlog for the current sprint. On Trello it is the **In progress** and **Completed** columns, and the taskboard in the logbook shows the same flow.

### Reflection

**Situation:** Once a sprint started, we needed to know exactly what we were committing to finish.

**Task:** I had to understand how to pick items from the product backlog for a sprint and track them to completion.

**Action:** We moved cards from the Product Backlog into In progress, such as designing the final ActiveEdge logo (6/7 tasks done) and creating the mood board for the brand identity. Finished work, such as the Sprint 1 market research, SWOT analysis and brand mission and vision statement, went into Completed with all tasks ticked (7/7).

**Result:** Keeping the sprint backlog small and visible made it easy to see what was done and what was still open, and it kept the team focused on the sprint goal.
